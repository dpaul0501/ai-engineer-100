Why this exists

AI can write the code. Your value is everything around it.

Understand

Know why the code works, down to the line, and what breaks when an assumption fails.

Verify

Prove it works with tests that fail when the code is wrong, not tests that only check shapes.

Ship

Put it in front of real people, learn from them, and earn trust in public.

What you walk away with

Proof, not a certificate

If you are a student

  • 100 interview problems, each taken past the solution to variations, failure cases, and real code
  • 14 builds on your public GitHub, each one defended live
  • 100 public posts: a track record recruiters can read
  • Evidence from your two paths: merged open-source work, a paper, hackathon placements, paying clients, or a product with users

If you are a working professional

  • The internals behind the AI tools you already use: attention, training, inference, RAG, agents
  • A disciplined way to work with coding agents: spec, review, eval, reject
  • A public portfolio for moving into AI and ML roles
  • Evidence from your two paths, for example open-source contributions, a paper, or paying consulting clients

The depth ladder

Solving the problem is the first rung

  • SolveWrite it yourself, with tests. State the complexity.
  • VarySorted input? A stream? k instead of 2? What changes?
  • Break itDuplicates, floats, Unicode, overflow, data bigger than memory.
  • ML lensTwo Sum with "close to target" becomes nearest-neighbor search.
  • In the wildTwo Sum is PostgreSQL's hash join. Read nodeHashjoin.c.

For ML/DL: learn the concept, read a real implementation, and discuss every parameter.

Coding with AI

The AI rules loosen as your judgment grows

Days 1–20AI explains, you type. AI may explain and review. It does not write your code.
Days 21–60AI pairs, you prove. AI writes code. Tests come first, and you explain every line.
Days 61–100AI agents, you review. You write the spec, review the PR, run evals, and reject bad output.
Every Sunday

The defense

  • You demo the weekly build to a moderator and your peers, on an input they choose.
  • They point at a line. You explain it and say what breaks if it changes.
  • You show the test that catches that break.
  • You say which parts AI wrote, and how you verified each one.

A peer group, not a class

Who you work with

Peers

Interview each other every Friday, review each other's PRs and posts, and question each other's builds on Sunday.

Moderators

Run the Sunday defenses, observe and score the peer mock interviews, and keep the standard high.

Technology leaders

Give feedback on your ideas at the reviews on Days 30, 60, and 90 and at demo day. Run industry mock interviews when they are available.

Idea review: 5 minutes to present, 10 minutes of feedback. Leave with one thing to stop, one to start, and one person to contact.

The mindset

Start preparing from Day 1. Do not wait for the degree.

A company pays you because your work is worth more to it than your salary. Thinking like an entrepreneur means knowing what that worth is, and growing it.

The old path was four years of study, then placement. Use those same years to build the profile that makes companies, clients, and collaborators want to work with you. If placement comes, you walk in with proof. If it does not, you already have it.

500 LeetCode problems and copied projects are not enough

What stands out now: work that strangers merged, judges ranked, reviewers accepted, or someone paid for.

The mindset

Believe it, then test it

Believe you can build something people want in 100 days. Then test that belief every week, at every level.

Code: tests that fail when the code is wrong
Models: evals with an error bar, not one lucky run
Understanding: a live defense, line by line
Ideas: interviews about what people already do
Product: usage logs from real people
Value: it gets merged, accepted, ranked, or paid for

Test the program the same way. Do Week 1 Day 1 today. If the depth ladder has not changed how you read code by Day 7, it is not for you.

Community

Four rules

Ship every day

A small honest post beats a perfect one that never goes out.

Be honest about AI

Say what AI wrote. Unverified code is not finished.

Lift two people a day

Useful comments on other participants' posts. This is how a cohort becomes a movement.

Share your work, not the labs

Post your own code and findings. The lab specs, tests, and defense questions stay in the cohort.
